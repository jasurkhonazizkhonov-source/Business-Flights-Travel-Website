## 1. Mobile usability

### 1.1 What was tested

* **Pages (every page type):** home, `/flights` (with "Add preferences" open), `/contact`, `/about`, `/how-it-works`, `/business-class`, `/destinations`, all five region hubs, five city pages, three articles, `/blog`, `/privacy-policy` and the other static pages: 24 representative routes (every static route, every hub, a sample of the 171 city pages and 36 articles, which share one template each). Structure of all 223 routes was also swept by the existing structural suite.
* **Viewports:** the page audit ran at 320, 360, 375, 390, 414, 430 and 768 px (portrait) and 1024 and 1280 px (desktop regression); the interaction checks ran at 320x700, 375x812, 390x844, 430x932, 768x1024 (portrait) and 568x320, 667x375 and 844x390 (landscape phones). Landscape was therefore covered by interaction, the cookie notice and the menu, not by the page-by-page overflow audit.
* **Method (not screenshots alone):** an audit script (`mobile-audit`, a scratch tool kept outside the repository) loaded each page at each size, scrolled it through, and measured horizontal overflow, tap-target size, overlapping or touching targets, clipped text, large sticky elements, duplicate controls and text under 12 px. An interaction script then drove the real controls at the eight sizes above: opened the mobile menu and its Destinations submenu, the date picker, the travelers popup, the airport suggestions, submitted an empty form (focus and error visibility under the sticky header), simulated a half-height screen for the on-screen keyboard, loaded a first visit with the cookie notice, and checked the contact form and newsletter. Screenshots of home, flights (default and preferences open), the menu, the footer, contact, a city page and an article were reviewed by eye. 178 interaction checks ran per pass (176 pass; the 2 that report are the documented 32 px calendar-day limit, section 1.4).
* **Regression test added:** `tests/mobile-usability.spec.ts` (7 tests) pins what follows.

### 1.2 What was found, and the exact minimal fix

| # | Problem (measured) | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The fixed "Best Deals" call pill sat over the right end of the fields being filled in, on every phone and small tablet (contradicting the component's own stated intent) | `FloatingDealsButton`: below 1024 px it also steps aside while a whole form (`data-hide-floating-cta-small`, set on the flight form and the contact form) is on screen, and comes back once the form is out of view. From 1024 px up nothing changes |
| 2 | **Verified defect:** the pill sits in the root layout and looked up the elements to avoid only once, so on a page reached by a link (not a fresh load) even the existing submit-button rule never applied | the lookup is redone on every route change (`usePathname`) and when the breakpoint is crossed |
| 3 | First-visit cookie notice: between 640 and about 700 px it switched to a side-by-side layout too early, squeezing its text into a narrow column: **52% of the screen at 640x900 and 88% of a landscape phone (667x375)** | the side-by-side layout now starts at 768 px (`md`); the notice is at most 40% of the screen at every size tested (640x900: 52% -> 15%; 667x375: 88% -> 36%; a test asserts at most 55%) |
| 4 | Header: menu button 40x40, logo link 28 px high | menu button 44x44; logo link 44 px high (the header height is unchanged) |
| 5 | Trip-type buttons 36 px, "Add preferences" toggle 32 px, traveler steppers 40 px | 44 px on a touch screen (below 640 px, and on any touch device by the `pointer-coarse` variant); a mouse desktop keeps the original sizes |
| 6 | 32 standalone links under 24 x 24 px (WCAG 2.2 AA 2.5.8): breadcrumb links (15-16 px), "Browse ... by country" (18 px), "See how a flight request works" (18 px), related-article links (18 px), phone and e-mail links on contact and about (19-20 px), home links (20 px), and a 28 px "Cookie Settings" and legal row | padding added without moving the layout where the link sits in a line (negative margins), or modest padding where it is a list row; home and contact CTAs `min-h-11` |
| 7 | Calendar day buttons 32 px on phones up to 380 px wide | **not changed** (see 1.4): a larger size was tried and reverted because it pushed the (invisible, closed) calendar's right edge past a 320 px screen and made the page scroll sideways |

Result of the same audit before and after at 375 px: tap targets under 24 px **35 -> 1** (the remaining one is an e-mail link inside a sentence, which the standard exempts); "small" (under 44 px, advisory) **about 125 -> about 60**; horizontal overflow, clipped text, text under 12 px and oversized sticky elements: **none, before or after**. No duplicate control was found: the two "Select date" buttons are the departure and return fields (each is named by its own label), and the header and page "Get a Free Flight Quote" links are in different places.

### 1.3 Desktop and tablet preserved

Every change is either a responsive class that applies below a breakpoint (`sm`, `md`, `lg`), the `pointer-coarse` variant (touch devices only), or a negative-margin padding that does not move layout. The audit at 1024 and 1280 px shows the same findings as before the change; the pill is asserted unchanged from 1024 px up; the whole UI suite ran at 767, 768, 769, 1023 and 1024 px, and the project's layout-stability test (the To field and submit button never move) is part of it. Two existing tests encoded the old pill behaviour (it returned as soon as the submit button left the screen, even with the form still showing, which is the overlap that was fixed): they now keep the original assertions at 1024 px and above and assert the new rule below it.

### 1.4 Remaining mobile limitations (not fixed, and why)

* **Calendar days are 32 px on screens up to 380 px wide** (44 px above): above the 24 px minimum but below the 44 px comfort size. The popover is positioned from its field and always mounted (closed, inert and transparent), so wider cells push its edge past a 320 px screen. A comfortable size would need the popover repositioned (for example full-width on phones): a layout change, left for your decision.
* The fixed pill still passes over page content while the visitor scrolls (it hides near primary CTAs, forms, popups and the cookie notice). That is inherent to a floating button; removing it is a design decision.
* Footer legal links and "Cookie Settings" are 36-38 px (above the minimum, below 44).
* **Not tested:** a real soft keyboard (only a half-height screen was simulated), real phones, Safari/iOS and Firefox (their engines are not installed here and were not downloaded), assistive technology on a phone.

## 2. Security and limitations re-examined

| Item | Outcome | Evidence and what remains |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Trusted Cloudflare IP** | **Improved and verified; activation needs your approval** | Re-read `src/lib/client-ip.ts` and every consumer (IP capture, location, country hint, rate-limit key): all four use the same per-request decision. Without a proof only `CF-Connecting-IP` (and `CF-Connecting-IPv6` for Cloudflare's stand-in range) is read; `X-Forwarded-For`, `X-Real-IP` and `Forwarded` are never read in Cloudflare mode. With `EDGE_PROOF_SECRET` set, a request without the exact secret gets no trusted IP, no location and no country. Added tests (now 11 pure + 3 on a real server): a proven request with a malformed, private, two-valued or garbage address still yields no IP; a repeated proof header (`secret, secret`), and the secret sent in any other header, are refused; the secret is never reflected in any response body or header of the public routes. **Corrected from the previous report:** if the secret is set before the Cloudflare rule exists, the forms do **not** simply "keep working": every visitor shares one rate-limit bucket (the whole site gets one client's allowance, 6-8 per 10 minutes) and visitors are refused after that (now measured in a test and stated in the docs). A container on the tunnel or CRM network can still forge Cloudflare headers until the proof is activated. Exact steps (Cloudflare "Modify Request Header" rule setting `x-bft-edge-proof`; `EDGE_PROOF_SECRET` in `.env`, 24+ characters; verify a real submission; back out by emptying it) are in `IP_AND_LOCATION_CAPTURE.md` section 6. Nothing was enabled or generated |
| **B. Rate limiting** | **Verified by measurement; no code change (limitation documented); an architecture decision for you** | The limiter is one in-memory window per process, checked first in each of the three actions and the hint route (`tests/server-action-surface.spec.ts` asserts the limiter precedes validation and any outbound call). New `tests/rate-limit-topology.spec.ts` measures on real servers: **two instances do not share counts** (the same client gets the full allowance on each: scaling to N containers multiplies it by N; today there is one); **without the edge proof, rotating a forged `CF-Connecting-IP` is never limited**; **with the proof, forged rotation shares one bucket and is limited**, while a proven client is limited by its own address. No alternative route bypasses it (all three actions are the only RPC entry points; the manifest check below). A known property of the implementation: beyond 50,000 distinct keys the oldest are evicted first, so a flood of distinct real addresses could reset an older bucket. The CRM's database-backed per-visitor limits are the shared layer. No Redis, database or service was added; a shared store would be warranted only if more than one website instance is ever run, and needs your approval |
| **C. CSP** | **One safe hardening made; `unsafe-inline` remains required** | Added `script-src-attr 'none'`: inline event-handler attributes are refused (the site has none on any of its 223 pages, re-measured; the CSP experiment showed 0 violations across home, newsletter toast, flight form, calendar, airport list and contact; a new test proves an injected `onclick`/`onerror` neither runs nor goes unreported). `'unsafe-inline'` for scripts remains: 1,220 inline framework scripts in 61 forms on every page (the Turbopack bootstrap and the flight-data payload), which differ per page and per build; removing it breaks hydration (30 scripts blocked in the experiment). Next's experimental SRI hashes external files only. A nonce architecture needs per-request rendering of every page (no static generation, no PPR) and was not demonstrated to be needed. Styles: Sonner injects a `<style>` at run time and React/framer-motion set 5,926 `style=""` attributes, so `style-src 'unsafe-inline'` also remains. **The CSP is not fully hardened**; no directive was weakened |
| **D. Public forms, API routes, Server Actions, redirects** | **Verified; no new vulnerability** | New `tests/server-action-surface.spec.ts` (9): the only `"use server"` files are the three public actions, each exporting exactly one function; the production build's server-reference manifest registers exactly those three (and none on the edge); exactly two routes exist (`/api/health`, `/api/visitor-hint`), GET only, copying no request header into an answer; every redirect destination is a fixed path or the one fixed canonical host with the same path (no destination takes a host or URL from the request); no `NEXT_PUBLIC_` variable carries a key, secret, password or token. Existing `http-boundaries` (7), `security-headers` and the CRM-contract tests also pass. Server-side validation, fixed error messages, honeypot, minimum fill time and idempotency are unchanged |
| **E. Date flexibility** | **Verified** | The website's request keys (re-extracted from the code) are all in the CRM's intake schema, including `dateFlexibilityDays` (integer 0-3, implying the Boolean; contradictions refused). Cached old forms: the legacy Boolean is kept without inventing a number (tests). Exact dates is the default; invalid values (4, -1, 1.5, "2", null, true, NaN) are refused by the server; e-mail and confirmation show the choice; one control, in the preferences section, kept when it is collapsed. **Dependency:** the CRM side (commit `831d87f`) exists only locally and is **not on the CRM remote** (`e132c2e`): the website must not be deployed against a CRM that lacks it, and the CRM migration `20261123000100` must be applied first (`DATE_FLEXIBILITY.md` section 5) |
| **F. Device location** | **Verified** | All eight guarantees re-read against the code and the 45 location tests per project, which ran again (section 3): no coordinates in any request, URL, storage or log; a manual choice beats a late result; denied, unavailable, inaccurate, no-airport and unsupported leave the form working; no prompt on other pages; no gap found, so no test was added |
| **G. Privacy wording** | **Accurate to the code; legal review still required** | The text separates the browser's device location (used only in the browser) from the network-derived estimate (Cloudflare, optionally completed by the CRM's local database), and calls the latter "not your address or your GPS position". **For counsel to review, not approved:** (1) the sentence that the CRM "may complete" a country-only location from an IP-location database on our server and that the address "is not sent to another company for this" (verified in the CRM code and its GeoIP document, but the CRM is a separate system and its production installation of that database is itself undone); (2) the statement that the device position "is not sent to us, not stored by us and not recorded with your request"; (3) the description of Cloudflare's processing; (4) whether a cookie-consent and retention statement is required for your jurisdictions. No legal compliance is claimed |
