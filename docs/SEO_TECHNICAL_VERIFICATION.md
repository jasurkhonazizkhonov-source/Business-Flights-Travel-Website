# SEO technical verification - release candidate

Date: 2026-11-20. Branch `review/website-parity-and-contact-ip-2026-11-15` (uncommitted working tree). Evidence: the local production (standalone) build served on 127.0.0.1:3101, `scripts/validate-seo.mjs`, `scripts/audit-seo-inventory.mjs`, the Playwright suites, and read-only public GETs of the live site for comparison. **Nothing was deployed.** "Lab" numbers are not field data; no Core Web Vitals from real users were available.

`validate-seo`: **223 routes, 0 failures** (HTTP 200, no redirect, self-canonical, no `noindex`, one H1, title and description present, sitemap membership, robots). Inventory audit: 0 non-200, **0 duplicate titles, 0 duplicate descriptions, 0 duplicate H1s**, 0 broken internal links, every route reachable from the home page, maximum depth 2 clicks. (A duplicate title between the Africa hub and the Africa planning article was found by the audit and fixed before this verification: hubs are now titled "Business-Class Destinations in X".)

## 2. Canonicals

* Every indexable route: exactly one `<link rel="canonical">`, equal to its own production URL. Checked on all 223 routes (the sitemap and the canonicals use the same hostname).
* `/flights?to=XXX` (58 reported URLs, and any other value): canonical `/flights`; the clean destination page is the one meant to rank. No canonical chains, none pointing at a redirect, a 404, or the home page.
* `og:url`, Twitter and JSON-LD URLs agree with the canonical (`pageMetadata` builds them from one path; the destination and hub pages build theirs from `destinationPath` / `/destinations/<region>`).

## 3. robots.txt

Live today (read-only): `User-Agent: *`, `Allow: /`, `Disallow: /api/`, `Sitemap: https://www.businessflights.travel/sitemap.xml`.
This build: the same plus **`Disallow: /cdn-cgi/`** (Cloudflare's namespace on this host; its email-address obfuscation links to `/cdn-cgi/l/email-protection`, which Google reported as a 404). Nothing indexable is blocked: blog, destinations, flights, the CSS/JS under `/_next/` and all pages stay open (`tests/seo.spec.ts`, two tests).

## 4. Sitemap

223 `<loc>` entries: 11 static + 5 hubs + 171 destinations + 36 articles. Excluded and tested: query URLs (`?to=`), redirected URLs (`/terms`, the retired blog slugs), 404 URLs (`/offers`), assets and image endpoints (`/twitter-image`, icons, manifest), anything under `/api/`. `lastmod`: **not emitted for static pages, hubs and destinations** (no real per-page modification tracking exists; stamping every URL "now" on each deploy is the anti-pattern Google warns about); **emitted for articles** from `updatedAt`, a hand-maintained date, which this pass advanced to 2026-11-20 only for the ten articles that really changed.

## 6. Metadata

Titles, descriptions and H1s are unique across all 223 routes (inventory audit). Open Graph and Twitter tags are generated by one function (`pageMetadata`) or, for destinations and hubs, the equivalent in `generateMetadata`; the share images are the branded `opengraph-image`/`twitter-image` routes (served as images, excluded from the sitemap). ## 8. Internal linking

Hierarchy: Home -> Destinations -> **Region hub** -> country section -> city. Every destination page is linked from the destinations index, from its region hub (if it has one), from related-destination blocks, and from articles where relevant. Incoming internal links per destination page: min **4** (was 3), median **6** (was 5). Articles: related links plus guides on destination pages. The 58 `/flights?to=` links stay as the quote call to action (canonical `/flights`).

## 9. Rendering and crawlability

The page content is in the server-rendered `<main>` (no loading fallback, no content swapped in by a script: tested on the home page, an article, a destination and a hub); `Reveal` leaves the first screen exactly as rendered (documented in the component); breadcrumbs, links, metadata and JSON-LD are in the initial HTML; hub and destination pages work without JavaScript for content discovery. No page requires a click before its text appears.

## 11. Post-deployment Search Console checklist (nothing here is automated; none of it guarantees indexing)

1. Confirm the deployed canonical host: `https://www.businessflights.travel`, and that `http(s)://businessflights.travel` redirect to it.
2. Open `https://www.businessflights.travel/sitemap.xml`: 223 URLs, all `https://www...`.
3. Submit (or resubmit) the sitemap.
4. URL Inspection on a handful of representative URLs: the home page, `/destinations/europe`, one destination (e.g. Munich), one expanded article.
5. Check the rendered HTML and the selected canonical in the inspection result.
6. Confirm no `noindex` and that robots.txt now shows `Disallow: /cdn-cgi/`.
7. Request indexing for a **small** number of high-priority corrected pages only (hubs, a few featured destinations, the expanded articles). Do not mass-submit.
8. Re-check the indexing report after Google has re-crawled (days to weeks): the "noindex" and "404" groups should drain; "Discovered" depends on Google's crawl scheduling.
9. Watch for new errors (soft 404, canonical conflicts) after the hubs and redirects go live.
10. Record the date of deployment so later changes can be compared.

## 14. Lab LCP investigation (2026-11-22; lab emulation on the local production build; not Lighthouse, not field data)

Lighthouse is not installed on this machine and was not installed. The measurement is the project's own Chromium emulation (`Playwright` + Chrome DevTools Protocol): **mobile** = Pixel 7 profile (390x844, device pixel ratio 2.6), 4x CPU slowdown, 150 ms RTT, 1.6 Mbit/s down; **desktop** = 1350x900, no CPU slowdown, 40 ms RTT, 10 Mbit/s. Median of 3 cold loads per page. The page is served by a local HTTP/1.1 Node server (no HTTP/2 prioritisation, no CDN, no caching), so the numbers are a harsh, pessimistic proxy: **they are not real-user Core Web Vitals and must not be quoted as such.** Real data needs the deployed site (PageSpeed Insights / CrUX / Search Console "Core Web Vitals").

| Page (mobile emulation) | LCP | LCP element | FCP | TTFB | CLS | JS (files) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `/` | 2.2 s | dimmed hero photo (45 KB) | 1.9 s | 15 ms | 0.000 | 588 KB (25) |
| `/flights` | 2.6 s | text (hero paragraph) | 2.6 s | 32 ms | 0.000 | 588 KB (25) |
| `/destinations` | **5.5 s** | a destination-card photo (129 KB) | 1.7 s | 19 ms | 0.000 | 225 KB (20), **1.6 MB of images** |
| `/destinations/europe` | 1.4 s | text | 1.4 s | 10 ms | 0.000 | 225 KB (20) |
| `/destinations/europe/germany/munich` | 3.6 s | dimmed hero photo (131 KB) | 1.3 s | 9 ms | 0.000 | 225 KB (20) |
| `/blog/business-class-vs-first-class` | 3.6 s | article hero photo (176 KB) | 1.2 s | 8 ms | 0.000 | 225 KB (20) |
| `/contact` | 2.0 s | text | 2.0 s | 11 ms | 0.000 | 330 KB (24) |
| desktop emulation, all seven pages | 0.32-0.74 s | - | 0.32-0.59 s | 7-124 ms | 0.000 | - |

**What the measurements show (causes, not guesses):**
* **TTFB is not the problem** (7-32 ms; the pages are static), and **CLS is 0.000 on every page** after the layout-shift fix of the previous task.
* **The LCP element is discovered immediately**: the hero image is requested within about 0.3 s of navigation on the article, destination and home pages (`priority` / `fetchPriority="high"` work). The time is spent **transferring bytes through a 1.6 Mbit/s pipe shared with the JavaScript and the other images**: on the article page about 660 KB (HTML + 225 KB JS + 13 KB CSS + 398 KB images) must cross 200 KB/s before the hero finishes, which is almost exactly its 3.6 s LCP. So LCP in this emulation is **bandwidth-bound**; the lever is fewer or smaller bytes before the hero completes.
* **`/destinations` is the outlier (5.5 s)**: the page lists 171 destinations and the browser, which loads lazy images within a larger distance on a slow connection, pulls **1.6 MB of card photos**; the LCP card image's request was queued behind them (it started at 2.4 s). On a real HTTP/2 connection with image priorities this queueing is much milder, but the page weight is real.
* The home and `/flights` JavaScript (588 KB in 25 files) includes the quote form (calendar, passenger popup, phone input, animation library); main-thread long tasks add up to 1.0-1.5 s at 4x CPU on those two pages and 0.1-0.6 s elsewhere.

**Change made (the only one with a measurable effect):** the four *dimmed decorative* hero photos (home, about, business class, destination detail: `alt=""`, shown at 45-60% opacity under a navy scrim) are encoded at quality 55 instead of 75 (`images.qualities: [55, 75]` in `next.config.ts`; every other image keeps 75). Measured on the LCP resource: **home 45 -> 36 KB (-20%) on mobile, 88 -> 70 KB on desktop; Munich 131 -> 105 KB (-20%) on mobile, 229 -> 184 KB on desktop**. The LCP *times* of the two builds lie inside the run-to-run noise of this emulation (home mobile 2.2 s before and 2.8 s after, with FCP moving by the same 0.5 s on an unrelated load spike), so **no LCP improvement is claimed**, only the byte saving; the visible quality at that opacity is unchanged to the eye.

**Not changed, and why:** the article hero is a full-visibility editorial photo, so lowering its quality would be visible; reducing the 1.6 MB on `/destinations` (for example loading fewer cards before scrolling, or splitting the page by region, which the new region hubs already do) and code-splitting the quote form's JavaScript off the home page are real redesign-level changes with trade-offs for the visitor and were left for a decision after field data exists. **Recommended next step:** after deployment read the 28-day field LCP/INP/CLS per page type in Search Console and PageSpeed Insights; only if field LCP exceeds 2.5 s on destination or article pages, take the two actions above, in that order.

## 15. Flight-form layout stability across widths (2026-11-22)

**Finding.** Between about 640 and 800 px the departure field's label row holds one or two lines depending on its contents, and the fields below follow it. Two events used to change it: the helper button appearing about 300 ms after load (+16 px) and, between 767 and 769 px, the button giving up its text when the suggestion is placed (-16 px; the To field is bottom-aligned, so it moved up 16 px). The tablet suite caught the second one (the test measures just before the suggestion arrives) and an earlier probe of mine missed it because its mocked answer used the wrong field name, so the suggestion was never applied: this is why the first fix (an invisible placeholder for the button) was not enough.

**Fix.** The label row reserves the two-line height for exactly the zone where it can hold two lines (`sm:max-[819px]:min-h-8` in `AirportAutocomplete.tsx`); the placeholder stays as a harmless extra. **Measured after the fix:** 127 widths (300-1500 px in 10 px steps plus 767-769 and 1023-1025), suggestion applied: **no shift of the To field or the form at any width**, and the row height is identical before and after the suggestion everywhere (84 px in the zone below about 820 px, 68 px elsewhere). Cost: in the 640-819 px range the first-flight label row is 16 px taller than strictly needed at the widths where its contents would have fitted one line.

**Regression test.** `tests/layout-stability.spec.ts` loads `/flights` at 25 widths around the form's breakpoints with the answer held back until the form is hydrated and the button showing, applies the suggestion, and asserts that the To field and the submit button did not move and that nothing overflows sideways.
